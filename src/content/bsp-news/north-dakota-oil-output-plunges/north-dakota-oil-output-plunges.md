<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>The North Dakota Department of Mineral Resources (DMR)&nbsp;reported last week that the state's oil output dropped more than 50% in April.</p><p><a href="/bsp-news/news/has-the-bakken-peaked?rq=production">Related: Has the Bakken Peaked?</a></p><p>During the month of April, North Dakota pumped 1,041,007 barrels of oil per day (bpd), 70,414 bpd less than March, according to the North Dakota Department of Mineral Resources (DMR).&nbsp;</p><p>Low crude prices and bad weather are being blamed on the record drop in production as producers are were forced to cut back the drilling and fracking of new wells.</p>

  <p>Currently, There are only 28 drilling rigs in the state compared to 77 one year ago. Other important stats for North Dakota include:</p><ul dir="ltr"><li>1,590 inactive wells during April (up from 67 in March)&nbsp;</li><li>Zero drilling rigs operating in Williams County, home to Williston</li><li>66 drilling permits were issued in April and 42 in May</li><li>Natural gas production fell from 1.7 million cubic feet per day (cf/d) in March to 1.6 million cf/d per day in April</li></ul><p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.dmr.nd.gov/oilgas/directorscut/directorscut-2016-06-15.pdf">Read more at dmr.nd.gov</a></p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p><br>&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p>
