<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>This week, Halliburton announced first quarter losses of $643 million and acknowledged that they have cut 9,000 jobs over the past two quarters.</p><p>The Houston-based oil giant has been forced to scale back operations in light of plummeting energy prices. They confirmed on Monday that the number of layoffs represent approximately 10% of their workforce.</p><p>Related: <a href="http://bakkenshale.com/bsp-news/news/halliburton-closing-minot-facility?rq=Halliburton%20Closing%20Minot%20Facility"><strong>Halliburton</strong> Closing Minot Facility</a></p><p>&nbsp;</p>
</div>

  <blockquote data-animation-role="quote" data-animation-override="">
    <span>“</span>Acting CFO Christian Garcia said “We are continuing to take a hard look at our operations and additional actions will likely be required in the second quarter. We believe that the long-term prospects of the industry remain sound. We are excited about the pending Baker Hughes transaction, which will significantly enhance the growth potential of our organization.<span>”</span>

  <p>In light of the $35 billion takeover of Baker Hughes, Halliburton is selling several business to secure the necessary regulatory clearance. Once the merger is approved by antitrust regulators, the new entity will surpass Texas-based Schlumberger as the world’s largest oil drilling company.</p><p>Related: <a href="http://bakkenshale.com/bsp-news/news/halliburton-to-merge-with-baker-hughes?rq=Halliburton%20to%20Merge%20With%20Baker%20Hughes"><strong>Halliburton</strong> to Merge With Baker Hughes</a></p><p>Read more at <a target="_blank" href="http://www.halliburton.com/public/news/pubsdata/press_release/2015/Hal-1Q15-Earnings-Release.pdf">halliburton.com</a></p>
