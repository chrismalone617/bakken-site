<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>Protests continue to grow around the country over the 1,172 mile Dakota Access Pipeline.</p><p><a href="/bsp-news/news/violence-erupts-at-pipeline-protest">Related: Violence Erupts over Pipeline</a></p><p>Construction on the Dakota Access Pipeline has been the focus of escalating protests since construction was halted over the Labor Day weekend after violence erupted.&nbsp;</p><p>Over the past three months, there have been three protests in Dallas, the home of the company behind the pipeline,&nbsp;Energy Transfer Partners.&nbsp;In September, dozens of people gathered in front of Energy Transfer's corporate headquarters and this past week,&nbsp;protesters yelled concerns at attendees at a fundraiser in Klyde Warren Park.&nbsp;</p><h2>Other Protests</h2><ul dir="ltr"><li>Yesterday, more than 800 people protested in Los Angeles' MacArthur Par.&nbsp;&nbsp;showed support for the pipeline activists. Among the crowd were celebrities including Susan Sarandon and Mark Ruffalo.</li><li>Over the weekend, protestors set up a camp in Cannonball ND,&nbsp;directly in the path of the project, according to Fox News. The groups erected tents and teepees on private land that the group contends rightfully belongs to native Americans.</li><li>In Morton County, ND, more than 127 people were arrested while Sheriff;s used pepper spray on the crowd.&nbsp;</li></ul>
