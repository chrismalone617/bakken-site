<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>North Dakota oil production increased 4% from a little more than 875,000 b/d in July to more than 911,000 b/d in August. Natural gas production rose ~3% and eclipsed 1 Bcf/d for the first time.</p><p>That means North Dakota accounts for more than 15% of all the oil produced and 1.5% of all the natural gas produced in the onshore U.S.</p><p><strong><a href="http://bakkenshale.com/bsp-news/news/largest-monthly-bakken-oil-production-increase-ever-july?rq=Largest%20Monthly%20Bakken%20Production%20Increase%20Ever%20in%20July">Largest Monthly Bakken Production Increase Ever in July</a></strong></p><p>Bakken production increased by more than 4% or ~35,000 b/d in in August to reach 847,000 b/d. That represents significant growth over volumes of ~638,000 b/d in August 2012 and ~379,000 b/d in August of 2011.</p><p>Other notes from the August monthly NDIC Director's Cut include:</p><ul><li>287 wells were permitted</li><li>9,452 wells producing</li><li>Average crude oil price of ~$93/bbl in August</li><li>Completions fell from 251 in July to 130 in August</li><li>Time from spud to total depth averaged 22 days</li><li>Time from spud to first completion rose from 79 to 105 days</li><li>About 450 wells are waiting to be completed</li></ul><p>August completion levels falling should not be alarming. July completion figures were inflated by bad weather and other completion delays in prior months.</p><p>Read the full director's cut at dmr.nd.gov</p>
