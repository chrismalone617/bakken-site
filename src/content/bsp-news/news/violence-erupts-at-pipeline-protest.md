<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>Construction on the Dakota Access Pipeline has come to a halt as demonstrations turn violent over the holiday weekend.&nbsp;</p><p>Related: <a href="http://www.mineralweb.com/news/dalrymple-at-odds-with-sioux-tribe-over-pipeline/">Dalrymple at Odds with Sioux Tribe Over Pipeline</a></p><p>What had been peaceful protests in the small town of Cannonball Dakota turned ugly after the pipeline company allegedly used bulldozers to destroy sacred tribal sites. Things escalated with guards using pepper spray and dogs to curb the situation.</p><p>Tribe spokesman Steve Sitting Bear told <a target="_blank" href="http://www.cbsnews.com/news/dakota-access-pipeline-protest-turns-violent-in-north-dakota/">CBS news</a> that six people had been bitten by dogs, including children.&nbsp;The oil company accused protesters of escalating the situation by breaking through a fence an attacking workers.</p><p>On Tuesday, U.S.&nbsp;Judge James Boasberg granted part of a temporary restraining order effectively halted work for the time being.&nbsp;</p><p>Demonstrators are concerned about the environmental impact of the pipeline, including contamination of the Missouri River, which is the primary water source for the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe. Tribal leaders are also upset that the pipeline will disturb sacred burial grounds.&nbsp;</p>
