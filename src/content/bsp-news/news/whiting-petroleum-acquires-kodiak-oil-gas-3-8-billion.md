<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>Whiting Petroleum announced on July 13, 2014, that it would acquire Kodiak Oil &amp; Gas, in an all stock transaction, for $3.8 billion.&nbsp;The deal makes the combined company the largest Bakken/Three Forks producer, unseating Harold Hamm's Continental Resources from the top spot.</p><p>Next to the <a target="_blank" href="http://eaglefordshale.com/">Eagle Ford Shale</a>&nbsp;in South Texas, the Bakken Shale is the most prolific shale play in the world, with daily oil production exceeding 1-million b/d. With Whiting's acquisition of Kodiak, the company is positioning itself to be an even more formidable force in the Bakken.</p><p>In the first-quarter of 2014, Whiting and Kodiak had combined production of 107,000 boe/d, and officials indicate total 2014 production will be 152,000 boe/d. The combined company has 855,000 net acres and an inventory of 3,460 net drilling locations.</p>

  <p>Whiting officials said the deal is valued at $6 billion when Kodiak's net debt of $2.2 billion is absorbed.</p><p>Kodiak shareholders will receive 0.177 share of Whiting stock in exchange for each of Kodiak common stock they hold, representing a value of $13.90 per share based on the closing price of Whiting shares on July 11, 2014.</p><p>The transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2014.</p><p>Read more at <a target="_blank" href="http://phx.corporate-ir.net/phoenix.zhtml?c=147759&amp;p=RssLanding&amp;cat=news&amp;id=1946925">whiting.com</a></p>
