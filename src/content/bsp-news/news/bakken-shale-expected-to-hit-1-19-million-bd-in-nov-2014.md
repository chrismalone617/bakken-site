<div data-sqsp-text-block-content="">
  <p>The Bakken Shale is expected to produce 1.193-million b/d of oil in November, up 22% from the same time last year, according to the <a target="_blank" href="http://www.eia.gov/petroleum/drilling/pdf/dpr-full.pdf">Energy Information Administration's</a>&nbsp;(EIA) Drilling Productivity Report. October's total production from the play is expected to be 1.164-million b/d.</p><p>Despite lower crude prices, EIA data shows gains in production across all major U.S. shale fields. According to the <a target="_blank" href="http://www.iea.org/">International Energy Agency</a> (IEA), only 4% of U.S. shale production needs prices above $80 for drillers to break even. In April of 2014, consultancy <a target="_blank" href="http://public.woodmac.com/public/home">Wood Mackenzie</a> issued a report that estimated break-even costs in the Bakken based on sub-plays. Break-even rates in the Sanish basin, one of the better areas for development in the Bakken, are estimated to average $58/bbl, according to the Wood Mackenzie report. Break-even costs were higher in other areas of the play.</p><p>Bakken operators continue the trend to be more efficient. EIA data for October shows new-well oil production per drilling rig is at 530. This is expected to increase to 537 by November.</p><h2>Gains in Texas</h2><p>In the <a target="_blank" href="http://eaglefordshale.com/">Eagle Ford Shale</a> in South Texas, and the Permian Basin in West Texas, gains were similar to the Bakken on a month-to-month basis.</p><p>The EIA estimates the Eagle Ford will produce 1.614-million b/d of oil November, up 29% from the same time last year. That's also a ~2% increase from October's expected production of 1.579-million b/d.</p><p>The Permian's October production is expected to be 7.765-million b/d, and increase ~2% to 1,807-million b/d in November.</p>
